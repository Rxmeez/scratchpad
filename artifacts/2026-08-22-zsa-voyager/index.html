ZSA Voyager: Is the Low-Profile Split Keyboard Worth It?

2026-08-22 · zero_rxpc · hardware / buying guide

The Voyager is ZSA's third keyboard (after the ErgoDox EZ and Moonlander): a split, columnar-staggered, ultra-thin board built around low-profile Kailh Choc switches instead of the tall MX switches most mechanical keyboards use. It's positioned as the "take it anywhere" ergonomic board — thin enough to live in a bag, but built to the same production quality as ZSA's desk-bound Moonlander.

Quick answer: if you want a compact, travel-friendly split keyboard with genuinely excellent software (Oryx + Keymapp) and don't need a deep tenting angle out of the box, the Voyager is the best in its category. If you already know you need aggressive tenting or a proper palm rest for wrist issues, budget extra for a tripod mount (~£70/$89) or look at a keywell board like the Glove80 instead.

Specs at a Glance

Layout

52 keys — number row, 3 alpha rows, 2 pinky columns, 4 thumb keys per hand. Column stagger, no key well.

Switches

Hot-swappable Kailh Choc v1 (low-profile). Choice of Red (linear 50gf), Pro Red (linear 35gf), White (clicky), or Brown (tactile) at order time — swappable any time after.

Thickness

~8mm case (excluding switches/keycaps) — noticeably thinner than the Moonlander, thin enough to slide flat into a backpack sleeve.

Keycaps

Double-shot PBT, Tai-Hao collaboration, half-pipe profile so RGB legends shine through. Large bundle of spare/alternate keycaps included.

Lighting

Per-key RGB LEDs with multiple built-in animations; can be dimmed or disabled entirely.

Connectivity

Wired only — USB-C to host, TRRS between halves. No Bluetooth (deliberate: keeps the case thin, avoids battery/interference issues).

Firmware

QMK (ZSA's fork), configured via Oryx (browser-based, no toolchain needed) and monitored live via the Keymapp desktop app.

Accessories

Steel bottom plate + alignment pins = magnetic accessory system. Ships with tenting pucks (5°); tripod mounts (~23-34° tenting) sold separately.

Price

$365 direct from ZSA (Taiwan), includes DHL/UPS express shipping, excludes import duties. No official UK/EU storefront — it's an import.

Warranty

2 years, plus ZSA's reputation for good long-term support and repairability.

Why It's Different From a Normal Keyboard

Two things define the Voyager's ergonomics:

  • Split halves — each hand gets its own board, positioned at shoulder width (or wider), which avoids ulnar deviation (the wrist-bending-inward posture a single flat keyboard forces on you).
  • Column stagger — instead of rows offset diagonally (the QWERTY layout inherited from typewriters), keys are lined up in columns matched to finger length. You reach keys by extending or curling a finger straight up/down, not by stretching diagonally.

On top of that, the Voyager uses Choc switches (1.5mm actuation distance) instead of standard MX switches (2.0mm), which shortens finger travel further — and lets ZSA make the whole case only 8mm thick.

Pros and Cons

Pros

  • Best-in-class build quality for a small ergonomic board — steel bottom plate, sturdy USB-C/TRRS ports, no wobble
  • Genuinely excellent software: Oryx (layout editor, flashes firmware over WebUSB) and Keymapp (live layout view + per-key heatmaps) are unmatched in this space
  • Hot-swappable switches — try Reds, Whites, Browns, or third-party Choc switches without soldering
  • Magnetic accessory system (tenting pucks, tripod mounts) snaps on/off in seconds, no screws
  • Comes with a proper travel case, spare keycaps/switches, multiple cable lengths — a complete kit, not just a bare board
  • Thinnest ZSA board yet — genuinely pocketable/bag-friendly compared to the Moonlander or a keywell board

Cons

  • No official palm rest that tents with the keyboard — most reviewers report wrist discomfort over long sessions without one, unless you can comfortably "hover-type"
  • Included tenting pucks only give 5° of tilt — basically decorative; meaningful tenting (20°+) needs the $89/~£70 tripod mount accessory
  • Pinky stagger is mild — pinkies don't naturally rest on the pinky key for all hand sizes
  • Wired only — no Bluetooth, so it's cable management every time you set it up on the go
  • $365 + import duties for UK/EU buyers (no local ZSA storefront) — expect landed cost closer to £320-£360 once duty/VAT is factored in
  • Steep learning curve if you're not already a touch typist — column stagger and a 52-key/layered layout take real adjustment time (days to months)

Who Should Actually Buy This

✅ Good fit: developers/writers who work from a laptop and want ergonomics without a bulky setup, people who travel with their keyboard, anyone who wants best-in-class configuration software and doesn't mind a learning curve.

❌ Think twice: if you have existing wrist/RSI issues and need serious tenting + palm support out of the box (budget for the tripod mount from day one, or look at a keywell board like the Glove80/Kinesis Advantage), or if you want Bluetooth/wireless.
